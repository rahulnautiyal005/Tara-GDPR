1. ICO fines can be substantial
For serious UK GDPR infringements, the statutory maximum can reach £17.5 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. The lower statutory maximum is £8.7 million or 2% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. (ICO)

That does not mean a startup that makes a mistake automatically gets a multimillion-pound fine. The ICO explicitly says its focus with SMEs is on helping organisations comply, and enforcement considers the circumstances. But inadequate security, unlawful processing, failure to respect people's rights and international-transfer violations can all fall within the ICO's enforcement powers. (ICO)

2. A data breach could be particularly damaging
Imagine a practitioner account gets compromised and someone downloads:

Customer name + phone number + DOB + birth details + consultation history + relationship/health/spiritual information.

That could be a significant breach.

Where a breach meets the reporting threshold, you generally have 72 hours after becoming aware of it to report it to the ICO. If it is likely to create a high risk to affected individuals, you may also have to tell those individuals without undue delay. You must also maintain breach records. (ICO)

This is why I marked access control, encryption, practitioner permissions and breach management as launch-critical.

3. The ICO can do more than fine you
A financial penalty isn't necessarily the worst outcome.

The ICO can require organisations to change how they process information, rectify or erase personal data, and comply with enforcement requirements. Failure to comply with enforcement or information requirements can itself result in further regulatory action. (ICO)

For an app business, an order affecting a core processing activity could be much more disruptive than a moderate fine.

If UK customer information is accessible by people outside the UK, including overseas support staff or developers, you need to assess whether UK international-transfer rules apply and establish the appropriate mechanism where necessary.

7. Even the ICO fee should not be ignored
If your company is required to pay the ICO data protection fee and doesn't, the ICO can impose a penalty. Current ICO guidance says the statutory maximum for non-payment can reach £4,350, with fixed penalties generally ranging according to the applicable tier. (ICO)
